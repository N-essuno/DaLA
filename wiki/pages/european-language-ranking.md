# Decision and scope

German, Czech, Ukrainian, Italian, Spanish, Estonian and French are the recommended first
resource-audit group. This is an agent research judgment from official project
pages, papers and repositories consulted on 2026-09-26, not a linguistic precision
measurement or a claim that implementation is production-ready. Adjacent ranks
within a tier are less meaningful than the differences between tiers.

Existing Danish, English, Dutch, Polish, Swedish, Bokmål, Nynorsk, Faroese and
Icelandic packs are excluded. Modern written languages are the focus; Russian and
Turkish are included as transcontinental European languages. This is a ranked
shortlist, not a proof of resource absence for every other European language.

Priority considers: (1) human-observed, separable grammar and spelling errors;
(2) diverse error families that admit locally defensible corruptions; (3) normative
lexicons/analyzers and explicit checker rules; (4) actual access/reuse prospects;
(5) likely integration effort. Corpus size alone does not determine rank. No
numerical scores are assigned because surviving safe rule counts have not yet
been measured. Corpus counts below are source sentences/texts, NOT prospective
DaLA output pairs or distinct safe substitution counts.

A = strongest next-pilot candidates. B = credible next wave with a material
access, filtering, or implementation gap. C = targeted investigation first.
Grammar/spelling columns below are proposed investigation families, not already
implemented or certified corruption rules.

# Portuguese reassessment: separate pt-PT and pt-BR

Reassessed 2026-09-26. Keep pt-PT at rank11/B and add pt-BR at rank12/B;
subsequent ranks move down one. This is a provisional agent assessment of resource
readiness, not a measured quality difference. French remains in tier A.

**European Portuguese:** COPLE2 provides naturally occurring learner writing with
corrections and a spelling/grammar/lexical annotation scheme. This gives pt-PT the
slight lead for mining context-conditioned error patterns. The paper does not
establish a fully annotated, unrestricted bulk correction-pair release: export
coverage, edit alignment, minimality and release-specific terms still need auditing.
Learner errors also need complementing with native-writer evidence. Lexical edits
must be rejected where they merely improve word choice.[^pt]

**Brazilian Portuguese:** the Penteado/Perez benchmark was manually constructed by
native speakers:102 grammar pairs,100 spelling examples,40 fast-typing examples
and40 internet-language examples. Its34 grammar sets chiefly concern confusable
words/expressions; they are not34 independent syntactic families. Exclude the
internet normalization category. The remaining242 candidate examples still need
inspection, including correction direction and correct-to-correct ambiguity; this
is neither a verified242-pair inventory nor natural error-frequency evidence.
Release access and data-specific reuse terms require checking.[^brgec]

Gimenes et al. studied nonword spelling errors in two Brazilian corpora, reporting
1,139 and1,260 error operations (not unique mappings or sentence pairs). Their
separate treatment of missing/wrong accents, cedilla, character edits and spaces
supports a Portuguese-specific spelling generator rather than importing English
error weights. The study excludes real-word errors; its aggregate statistics do
not establish an accessible licensed mapping list.[^brspell]

CoGrOO supplies a Brazilian grammar-checker implementation and evaluation modules;
its repository declares Apache2.0. Audit actual rules, embedded resources and test
examples before reuse. LanguageTool's Portuguese spelling infrastructure supplies
variant-specific dictionaries and replacement-pair mechanisms; shared Portuguese
rules cannot be counted twice as independent variant-specific evidence. Neither
checker acceptance nor dictionary membership proves sentence correctness.
[^cogroo] [^ptspell]

Essay-BR supplies professionally scored essays, not an established aligned minimal
correction corpus. It must not inflate the pt-BR correction-pair count.[^essaybr]
Carolina is a useful Brazilian clean-source candidate, with document-level licenses
and provenance; it still needs source selection, sentence-quality screening and
yield measurement. Its corpus header has separate NC-SA terms. No production-size
yield for either variety has been established here.[^carolina]

**Implementation recommendation:** share safe morphological primitives but maintain
separate pt-PT/pt-BR packs, source provenance, dictionary configuration, prompts,
held-out samples and acceptance tests. Start with conditioned subject/verb and
nominal agreement, then guarded contractions/crasis and verb/preposition patterns.
Clitic placement and register-sensitive constructions require especially strict
review. Spelling can cover accents, cedilla, character edits, observed mappings and
word boundaries; reject edits producing another accepted form unless syntax proves
an error. Preserve licensed orthographic alternatives and standard differences;
never use conversion between the varieties as a corruption. Candidate families
above are proposed work, not implemented or linguistically validated rules.

To reach tier A, pt-PT chiefly needs an audited usable annotated export and safe
pattern extraction; pt-BR needs broader independently checked grammar evidence
beyond lexical confusions, alongside a reusable spelling inventory. Both need
stratified corruption audits and variety-competent human review. This turn performed
primary-source agent inspection and documentation checks only; no human linguistic
validation, corpus extraction, implementation, builds or uploads occurred.

# How the resources fit DaLA

Correction corpora supply observed patterns, contexts and held-out evaluation.
New, separately licensed and quality-screened clean corpora supply the sentences
we corrupt. A small corrected corpus can therefore enable a large DaLA dataset;
it cannot establish the eventual clean-source yield. No new clean corpus was
screened or sized in this survey, and no 478,930-pair output is promised.

The preferred evidence order is human-corrected minimal edits, expert-authored
rules with positive/negative examples, then curated observed spelling mappings.
Explicit synthetic generators are useful implementations to audit, not evidence
that their error frequencies or every generated pair are valid. GitHub revision
mining is supplementary: code, names, English inside other languages, semantic
changes and non-GEC edits need removal.[^githubtypos]

LanguageTool rules combine multiple purposes. XML-rule totals must not be reported
as grammatical family counts; style, punctuation, formatting and duplicates have
to be filtered. A no-warning result does not certify grammaticality.[^lt]

# Access and correction scope findings

MultiGEC organizes 12 languages, but its distribution is research-only, personal
access and no redistribution. Obtain independently licensed originals when
available; do not put its distributed essays into a public HF dataset. Its
metadata also distinguishes minimal from fluency references: for example EIC
has minimal references whereas EKIL2 is listed under fluency. Never treat every
MultiGEC corrected text as a minimal gold sentence.[^multigec] [^multiterms]

Original MERLIN explicitly offers CC BY-SA 4.0; GECCC reports CC BY-SA 4.0;
UA-GEC explicitly offers CC BY 4.0 and a GEC-only branch. These are useful
counterexamples to assuming the MultiGEC terms apply identically to every
original corpus.[^merlin] [^cs] [^uk]

EKI lists CLARIN ACA restricted use. Repository software licenses do not by
themselves resolve every embedded corpus or lexicon right. In particular, the
DWDSmor Open Edition and full DWDS Edition differ; do not assume the full
lexicon is covered by the open code license. Pin and check exact resource releases
before producing a distribution.[^eki] [^dwdsmor]

FRIDA is documented, but this survey did not establish unrestricted bulk reuse.
COPLE2's annotation paper establishes a real three-way spelling/grammar/lexical
scheme, not that every corpus text is fully corrected. RONACC download links
are exposed by its authors; a complete download and artifact-specific license
audit is still pending.[^fr] [^pt] [^ro]

# Proposed acceptance gate for each next language

1. Pin original resource versions, annotation guidelines, terms and provenance.
   Freeze an independent evaluation portion before mining training patterns.
2. Catalogue error families separately from individual rules, observed mappings,
   lemmas and generated sentences. Mark empirical versus synthetic evidence.
3. As a planning target, try for at least8 distinct grammatical families and
   several spelling mechanisms, with50+ curated observed mappings when evidence
   permits. These are new proposed targets, not measured results or hard quotas:
   do not invent mappings or split near-identical rules to satisfy a number.
4. Generalize only with required syntax, morphology and lexical conditions.
   Query all analyses of a surface form; reject syncretism, alternate readings,
   licensed variants and transformations that merely change meaning.
5. For each family, test true-error cases and near-miss grammatical counterexamples.
   Simple edits are useful for attribution, but sentences need not be limited
   permanently to one error. Compose only audited compatible operations.
6. Inspect stratified generated samples plus a uniform sample, report ambiguity
   and source-error rates, and seek native linguistic review for uncertain cases.
   Agent inspection, automatic checks and human review must remain distinct.

Begin with German for integration practicality, Czech for broad observed-error
coverage, and Italian for a Romance pack with both learner and native-school
error evidence. Ukrainian is equally compelling where a clearly separated
GEC-only corpus matters most. Include Estonian early to test the architecture
beyond Indo-European morphology. Both Portuguese varieties are viable second-wave candidates; pt-PT has a slight
observed-error evidence lead. They need separate packs, prompts and evaluations.
The provisional order is not a requirement to finish one before investigating the other.

On 2026-09-26, the user confirmed moving French into the first implementation
group (tier A). Its provisional resource-readiness position remains7; this is a
priority promotion, not a new empirical quality result. French should be included
alongside the original six first-group languages. FRIDA supplies observed-error
leads, while Grammalecte and LanguageTool supply explicit rule resources.
FRIDA bulk access/reuse and contextual rejection of correct-to-correct homophone,
verb-form and participle substitutions remain open work. This decision does not
claim those checks have passed or authorize a new build by itself.

No new builds, resource-contact messages, or uploads were started by this survey.

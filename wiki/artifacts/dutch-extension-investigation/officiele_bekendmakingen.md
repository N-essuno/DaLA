# Dataset Card for Officiële Bekendmakingen

<!-- START-SHORT DESCRIPTION -->
Dutch parliamentary proceedings and government announcements from [officielebekendmakingen.nl](https://www.officielebekendmakingen.nl)
<!-- END-SHORT DESCRIPTION -->

Officiële Bekendmakingen is the Dutch government's official publication service.
It carries two things at once. The first is the historical record of parliament:
the *Handelingen* and *Kamerstukken* of the First and Second Chamber from 1814
onwards, digitised from some 2.4 million pages by the Koninklijke Bibliotheek in
the Staten-Generaal Digitaal project — verbatim debates, bills, explanatory
memoranda, budget chapters and committee reports. The second is the current
stream of statutory publication: laws and orders in the Staatsblad and
Staatscourant, together with the announcements municipalities, provinces and water
boards are required to publish. The result spans more than two centuries of Dutch
legislative and administrative language in a single collection.

### Data Fields

An entry in the dataset consists of the following fields:

- `id` (`str`): A unique identifier for each document.
- `text` (`str`): The content of the document.
- `source` (`str`): The source of the document (see [Source Data](#source-data)).
- `added` (`str`): The date when the document was added to this collection.
- `created` (`str`): The date range when the document was originally created.
- `token_count` (`int`): The number of tokens in the sample computed using the Llama 3 tokenizer.

## Additional Information

### Source Data

The documents were collected by GPT-NL from
[officielebekendmakingen.nl](https://www.officielebekendmakingen.nl), which
publishes both the historical parliamentary record and current official
announcements, and released as the `officiele-bekendmakingen` collection of the
[GPT-NL Public Corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GPT-NL/GPT-NL_Public_Corpus).

### License Information

Laws, decrees, and the publications of Dutch public authorities are free of
copyright under article 15b of the Dutch Copyright Act (Auteurswet). GPT-NL records
the licence of every document in this collection as public domain.

### Limitations

The nineteenth and early twentieth century material is recovered from scans of
printed proceedings, so it carries OCR errors and follows pre-1947 Dutch
orthography, while the modern material is digital and clean. The two read
very differently and are mixed together without a date field to separate them.
Documents are split per page range rather than per debate or per bill, so a single
sitting may run across many documents and a document may begin or end mid-sentence.
Statutory publication is structured, so standard legal phrasing repeats
across large numbers of documents.

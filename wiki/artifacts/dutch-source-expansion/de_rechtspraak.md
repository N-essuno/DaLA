# Dataset Card for De Rechtspraak

<!-- START-SHORT DESCRIPTION -->
Dutch court judgments published by [De Rechtspraak](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/)
<!-- END-SHORT DESCRIPTION -->

De Rechtspraak is the organisation of the Dutch judiciary, and publishes
selected judgments of the courts on rechtspraak.nl, each identified by an ECLI
reference. This subset holds those judgments, from district courts, courts of
appeal, the Council of State, the Central Appeals Tribunal and the Supreme Court,
across the full range of Dutch law: criminal, civil, administrative, tax,
immigration and family cases. Each document follows the structure of a Dutch
judgment — the parties and procedural history, the facts established, the
arguments of each side, the court's assessment, and the operative decision.

### Data Fields

An entry in the dataset consists of the following fields:

- `id` (`str`): A unique identifier for each document.
- `text` (`str`): The content of the document.
- `source` (`str`): The source of the document (see [Source Data](#source-data)).
- `added` (`str`): The date when the document was added to this collection.
- `created` (`str`): The date range when the document was originally created.
- `token_count` (`int`): The number of tokens in the sample computed using the Llama 3 tokenizer.

## Additional Information

### Source Data

The judgments were collected by GPT-NL from
[rechtspraak.nl](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/), where they are published as open
data under their ECLI identifiers, and released as the `rechtspraak` collection of
the
[GPT-NL Public Corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GPT-NL/GPT-NL_Public_Corpus).

### License Information

Judicial decisions are free of copyright under article 11 of the Dutch Copyright
Act (Auteurswet). GPT-NL records the licence of every document in this collection
as public domain.

### Limitations

Only a selection of Dutch judgments is published — cases are chosen for their legal
or public interest, so the collection is not a representative sample of what the
courts decide, and it over-represents appellate and precedent-setting decisions.
Judgments are anonymised before publication, which leaves them full of placeholders
such as `[verdachte]` and `[eiser]` in place of names. Criminal judgments could describe
offences, including violent and sexual offences, in explicit factual detail.
